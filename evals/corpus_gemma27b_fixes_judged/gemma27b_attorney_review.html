Gemma 27B legal answers, for attorney review

Gemma 3 27B (4-bit QAT) answered 100 questions on Israeli law and prepared 5 paralegal work files, using retrieval over the legislation corpus. Claude graded every answer against the eval's reference answers. Each item below shows the question or case file, the model's answer, the reference answer and Claude's assessment. Please mark whether you agree with the assessment and add a comment where the law or the grading is wrong.

Results

  • Short legal questions are mostly right. Rule recall, yes/no conclusions, section lookup and multiple choice all score 86–88%. All six trick questions (false premises, a request to hide assets from creditors, future legislation) were handled correctly.
  • Analysis of facts is the weak point. Applying the law to a fact pattern scores 42% and issue spotting 50%. The answers find the main statute but miss second issues: severance in a dismissal, the truth defence in defamation, the 24-hour limit on detention.
  • The case files contain errors a lawyer would have to catch. In the consumer case the file counts the 14 days from the order date and wrongly concludes the cancellation came a day late. In the tenancy case it adds up the landlord's deductions wrongly and says the lift levy was a proper charge. In the dismissal case the demand letter asserts that no permit was obtained, which the client doesn't know, and it prints her ID number. In the will case the objection is drafted for the wrong court. Graded the same way, the 12B files are no better on average (34.8 against 37.0). 27B writes full draft documents where 12B gave outlines, but it is weaker on dismissal and tenancy.
  • The automatic yes/no check misfired twice. It changed the model's correct “לא” to “כן” in IL-212 and IL-409, and fixed one wrong label in IL-219. Without it, yes/no accuracy would have been 16 of 18 instead of 15.
  • Citations are mostly right. 8 of 84 graded answers cite the wrong section for a correct statement. The most common case is the Notice Law, where s.4 (non-monthly workers) is cited in place of s.3. One answer invents a date (IL-198). Three issue-spotting answers end with a stray “</start_of_turn>” token.

How to review

For each question, read the model's answer next to the reference answer. Then say whether Claude's grade is right: Agree, Disagree or Unsure. Add a comment when you disagree, when the reference answer itself looks wrong, or when the model states something legally dangerous that the grade doesn't reflect. Your marks are saved in this browser only. When you're done, press Copy review and paste the result into an email.

Grades: Right means every key point is right and nothing material is wrong. Partial means some key points are missing or there is a minor error. Wrong means a wrong conclusion, a material legal error or no real answer. Multiple choice is marked automatically by the letter. Yes/no questions count the label for half and the explanation for half.

Paralegal case files

For each case the model got the full case file and was asked for a work file for the responsible lawyer. The work file has eight parts: facts, timeline, legal issues, deadlines, red flags, missing information, a draft document and the next step. Each part is scored out of its weight in the rubric (100 in total), and penalties are taken off for invented facts, wrong citations and forbidden conclusions.

How this was graded

The questions are a fixed sample of 100 from the 500-question Israeli legal eval (seed 2026), the same sample used for the 12B run on 29 September. Claude (Opus 5.5) graded each answer with the eval's own rubric (judge_prompt.md, prompt A for questions and prompt B for case files), against gold.jsonl and cases_gold.json. The model never saw these files. The overall score comes from the eval's unmodified score.py.

Two limits apply. The grader is a language model, and the eval's own guidance asks for a lawyer to check a sample before the numbers are relied on. That check is the purpose of this page. The case-file scores also involve more judgement than the question grades. The 12B case files were first graded in an earlier session at 42.8 on average. Re-graded in the same session and to the same standard as the 27B files, they average 34.8, and those re-graded scores are the ones shown here.
